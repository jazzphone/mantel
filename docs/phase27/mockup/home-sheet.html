The house tile, with the orange ring, is the new block. Escape goes back one level, then closes the sheet.

Every size: the house at a glance, then its rooms

Small is only a door into the sheet. From Tall up, the top of the block is House Status's: the house's name as the label, the one thing that matters most as the headline, the rest in a sentence. Under that, the bigger sizes add what House Status doesn't have, the rooms. Every part opens the sheet at that place: a category opens its page and a room opens that room. The lines scroll inside the block, as House Status's do. Tap any of them.

Decision 2, side by side

Both choices start the same way: you tap the house, and the sheet opens with the chips across the top. They only differ once you tap a chip such as Security, or a room's name. Use the "Chips and rooms open" switch above to try each one.

ChosenInside the sheet
  1. Tap Security. The sheet stays open, and Security slides in inside it.
  2. The title becomes "Security", and a "‹ Home" key appears next to the X.
  3. "‹ Home" goes back to the house list, where you were. X closes the sheet, and you're back on the grid.

You never leave the grid. It's a quick look and back, which is how Apple's works.

The screen's page
  1. Tap Security. The sheet closes, and the screen goes to its full Security page, the one the menu and tab bar already go to.
  2. That page fills the screen, with more room and the tab bar at its foot.
  3. To come back you use the menu or the tab bar's Home. The sheet isn't there anymore.

It's better for a long stay on one page. Each trip away from the grid costs a tap or two to get back.

Agreed: all five sizes3. Sizes, and House Status

Small, Wide, Tall, Large and Extra Large. Home doesn't replace House Status: it uses House Status's headline and wording, which come from the same chip model, so the two never disagree. Then it adds the rooms. A screen with a Large or Extra Large Home won't need House Status beside it, and a screen that wants only the alerts keeps House Status.

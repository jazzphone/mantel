Laundry on Mantel

A mockup of the Laundry card at every Home Grid size, its tile and chip on a generated room page, its detail sheet, and where it's set up. Pick a setup and a state below and everything on the page follows. It's drawn with the wall tablet's dark glass at real pixel sizes. Every number is made up.

The drawing

A front-loader drawn in the house glyphs' style, animated by what it's doing, the way the vacuum is. A combo uses the washer drawing; a dryer has a dial and no water. Badges sit at the upper right as on the vacuum: white for pause and delayed start, mint for done, red for a leak or an error. With Reduce Motion (or Motion off above) every state is still and reads from its badge and words.

Home Grid, every size

Small 196 × 196, Wide 405 × 196, Tall 196 × 405, Large 405 × 405 and Extra Large 614 × 405, on square cells. The headline is at the upper left wherever there is one. Small is HomeView's centered tile. Two machines share the block rather than needing two blocks. Pause and Stop only appear while the machine allows remote control; Clear appears on Done.

On a generated room page

The Laundry Room page gets each machine as a tall tile, as vacuums do. It lights while running and turns mint at Done. A Laundry chip joins every screen's chip row while a machine runs or waits to be unloaded, and goes away once it's cleared.

Detail sheet

Opens from the block, the tile or the chip. The drawing and the state are on the left; the cycle, its settings, what it used and what's around it are on the right. With two machines, a segmented control switches between them. Keys the machine won't accept are shown dimmed, with the reason underneath.

Setup, with no new settings page

Laundry becomes a kind under Accessories, next to Vacuums and Leak Sensors. Machines with their own integration are found automatically. A plug or a vibration sensor is added as a machine, the same way an outlet with a lamp can count as a light today. Each machine's settings live on its own accessory page.

How it reads any brand

Every source is reduced to one set of states before anything is drawn, so the card, chip, tile and sheet never know the brand. A machine's own integration is read from the entities on its device: the status and cycle-step sensors' raw values are matched to the shared states, and time left, finish time and progress are read whichever way the integration gives them. A machine with no integration is read from power, vibration, or both, by Mantel Server, which also keeps Done until it's cleared.
